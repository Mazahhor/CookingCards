Ten tips that actually get used.

01 🧂 Salt as you go Tap to flip ✓
Salt as you go

Season in thin layers while you cook, then taste at the end. One big dump of salt at the finish line tastes like an apology. Layers taste like cooking.

02 🔥 Heat the pan first Tap to flip ✓
Heat the pan first

Food hitting a cold pan sticks and steams. Let the pan get properly hot, then add the oil, then the food. That sizzle on contact is the sound of it working.

03 🔪 A sharp knife is the safe knife Tap to flip ✓
A sharp knife is the safe knife

Dull blades slip off tomatoes and into thumbs. Sharp ones go where you point them. Sharpen monthly, hone weekly, respect always.

04 🧄 Garlic burns fast Tap to flip ✓
Garlic burns fast

Onions take ten patient minutes. Garlic takes thirty seconds. Add it late, after the onions are done, or it turns bitter and the whole dish tastes of regret.

05 🥩 Pat it dry Tap to flip ✓
Pat it dry

Water is the enemy of crisp. Pat meat and veg dry before they hit the heat and they will brown instead of steam. Your tandoori char will thank you.

06 🍳 Don't crowd the pan Tap to flip ✓
Don't crowd the pan

Give everything room. A crowded pan traps steam and turns sear into stew. Two quick batches always beat one soggy one.

07 🌶️ Bloom spices in hot fat Tap to flip ✓
Bloom spices in hot fat

Flavour lives in fat. Thirty seconds of whole spices sizzling in oil pulls out more taste than ten minutes of boiling. That is the entire science of tadka.

08 🍋 Finish with acid Tap to flip ✓
Finish with acid

Flat dish? It probably needs acid, not salt. A squeeze of lemon, a splash of vinegar or a little tamarind at the end wakes everything up.

09 🧼 Clean as you go Tap to flip ✓
Clean as you go

Wipe the board, drop tools in the sink, bin the peels while things simmer. Future you, standing in a clean kitchen after dinner, will be genuinely moved.

10 🥄 Taste before you serve Tap to flip ✓
Taste before you serve

The five second insurance policy. Every professional kitchen does it and most home kitchens skip it. One spoonful before plating catches almost every mistake.
