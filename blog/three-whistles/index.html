What "Three Whistles" Actually Means

July 18, 2026 · 2 min read

If you grew up around an Indian kitchen, "cook it for three whistles" needs no explanation. If you did not, it sounds like the pressure cooker is reviewing the food.

Here is the short version. A stovetop Indian pressure cooker has a weight sitting on the vent. As pressure builds, steam eventually lifts the weight and escapes with a loud whistle, then the pot seals again and pressure builds back up. Each whistle is one full pressure cycle. Recipes count those cycles because they are a surprisingly reliable clock: nobody stands there with a timer, they just listen from the next room.

A rough translation table

Every cooker, stove and altitude behaves a little differently, but as a starting point:

  • 1 to 2 whistles. Tender vegetables, potatoes for mashing.
  • 3 whistles. The workhorse setting. Most dals, rice dishes, and both dal recipes that ship with Cooking Steps.
  • 4 to 6 whistles. Tougher legumes like whole chana, or meat that needs to give up and fall apart.

Translating for an Instant Pot

Electric pressure cookers never whistle, they just hold pressure quietly. The working conversion most cooks use:

  1. 3 whistles is roughly 10 to 12 minutes on high pressure. That is the number we use in the app's Dal Fry recipe.
  2. Let the pressure release naturally for dals and beans. A quick release is fine for vegetables, but dal foam can sputter through the valve, and natural release finishes the cooking gently.
  3. When in doubt, err shorter. You can always bring it back to pressure, but there is no undo for mush.

Why we kept whistles in the app

When we added recipes to Cooking Steps we deliberately wrote both forms, like "about 3 whistles on the stovetop, or 12 minutes on high in an Instant Pot." Grandmother units and machine units, side by side. Recipes are a language, and it felt wrong to translate away the accent.

Fun detail: the whistle count survives even when you switch the app between °F and °C, pounds and kilos. Some units are sacred.

If you want to hear it in action, any pot of dal on a stovetop is three whistles of proof.
