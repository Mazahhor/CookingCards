Your cooking journey, day zero

Cooking isn't hard.

Really. It never was, and it has never been easier than right now. There is a video for everything, a recipe for everything, and nobody on this planet was born knowing how to cook. Everyone you admire in a kitchen started once, badly, and simply kept going.

You just have to get started. That is the whole secret. That's it.

Day one

Burnt the toast. Ate it anyway.

Week two

Eggs: edible. Confidence: rising.

Month one

Cooked dinner without checking the recipe twice.

Sooner than you think

People start asking YOU for the recipe.

For the ones who just landed

New country, new degree, empty stomach.

Two suitcases, one laptop, zero recipes. It gets better.

Every July and August it happens. Thousands of students land somewhere new with two suitcases, one laptop and a phone full of goodbye photos. A different country for some, a different state for others, far enough either way that nobody is making dinner for you anymore. Masters, PhD, Bachelor's, MBA, Medicine, every degree there is. Brave about visas, brave about rent, brave about everything except dinner.

Because back home, dinner just appeared. Mom made it, or the hostel mess did, or the corner place that knew your order before you finished saying it. Nobody warns you that the hardest course this semester is feeding yourself on a Tuesday night, after a 6 pm lecture, in a kitchen the size of a cupboard, with one pan you bought for three dollars.

So week one is instant noodles. Week two is noodles with an egg, which honestly counts as growth. Week three you video call home and ask how the dal actually gets made, and someone says "bas, andaza se, just by feel" and hangs up. And you stand there thinking: by feel is not a measurement.

Here is the secret nobody tells you: that phone call IS the recipe.

Type it into the app while they talk, amounts and all, and it turns into steps you can follow at the stove with the flame already on. Cook it once, badly. Twice, better. By December you are the one hosting, your tiny kitchen smells like home, and everyone in your building assumes you always knew how.

Everything below, the gear, the five skills, works exactly the same in a cupboard-sized student kitchen. The noodle era can end this week.

It has never been easier.

Whatever generation figured out fire, respect. But this one gets the training wheels.

Failure is fully covered

Worst case, dinner burns and pizza exists. The stakes have literally never been lower in human history.

The only gear you actually need

One decent knife A big cutting board One wide pan One pot with a lid A wooden spoon A tasting spoon A kitchen towel

That is it. The drawer full of gadgets can wait years. An air fryer is the one luxury this app will keep nudging you toward, because it forgives almost everything.

Five tiny skills before any recipe

  1. Curl your fingers. Hold food with your fingertips tucked back like a claw, and the knife can only ever meet knuckle. Slow is fine. Slow with all ten fingers is the goal.
  2. Medium heat is your friend. Beginners burn things on high and give up. Nearly everything cooks happily on medium. High heat is a privilege you earn later.
  3. Taste as you go. Cooking without tasting is texting with your eyes closed. One little spoon, every few minutes, tells you everything.
  4. Boil vs simmer. A boil is angry bubbles everywhere. A simmer is a lazy bubble every second or two. Recipes care about the difference, so now you know it.
  5. Done is a number, not a colour. Chicken is safe at 165°F inside. When in doubt, cut the thickest piece open. Clear juices, no pink, you are fine.

Beginner truths to keep on the fridge

  • If it tastes boring, it usually needs salt or lemon, not more spices.
  • Burnt garlic cannot be saved. It costs thirty seconds to start over. Start over.
  • Onions taking forever is not you failing, it is onions being onions.
  • Nobody's first roti is round. Nobody's.
  • A recipe is a map, not a law. Yours can taste like you.
And that is genuinely all of it

You just have to get started.

Not tomorrow, not after one more video. Tonight counts. Say it with the button and the kitchen is yours.
